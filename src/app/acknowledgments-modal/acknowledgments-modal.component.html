Acknowledgments

This online resource has been designed to present, explain and 'bring to life' the four paintings by William Hogarth that comprise his 'The Humours of an Election' series. Painted in 1754-55, they have been in Soane's collection since 1823.

This project was made possible by a Follow-On grant from the Arts and Humanities Research Council (AHRC), running from March to July 2025 ('The Humours of an Election: Annotation, Animation, Education', AH/Z507556/1). It developed from an earlier AHRC grant, 'Eighteenth-Century Political Participation and Electoral Culture' (ECPPEC) that ran from 2020 to 2023, the main output of which can be found at https://ecppec.ncl.ac.uk/.

Both projects were led by Professor Matthew Grenby (Newcastle University), with co-investigators Professor Elaine Chalus (University of Liverpool) and Dr. Tom Schofield (Newcastle). Dr. Kendra Packham worked on the project as Senior Research and Innovation Associate, and Dan Foster-Smith as Research Software Developer (both Newcastle). Foster-Smith and Schofield developed the online platform, AI-generated animations and interactive functions. Chalus, Grenby and Packham wrote the annotations for the paintings. The scripts for the animated characters were written by Grenby and Packham, and sometimes quote from 'A Poetical Description of Mr Hogarth's Election Prints: in Four Cantos' published in 1759, apparently 'under Mr Hogarth's Sanction and Inspection'.

The project was developed in partnership with Sir John Soane's Museum, with particular support from Joanna Tinworth, Curator, (Collections) (who has written extensive catalogue entries for the Soane's Hogarth paintings); Rhiannon Litterick, Public Programmes & Young People Manager; Morag Calderbank, Learning Manager; Tallulah Smart, Learning Officer; Liz Ison, Picture Library Assistant; Tilly Buckroyd, Digital Communications Manager and Dell Forrester, IT Operations Manager. New, high-resolution digital photographs of the paintings were taken by Eva Herzog as part of the project.

Other than the images of the paintings, the images of other original artworks used in this resource either belong to Sir John Soane's Museum, are in the public domain, or have been used with the permission of the owners. The engraving of 'Hogarth Painting the Comic Muse', (1764), © Sir John Soane's Museum, London was animated for the resource's introduction with the permission of Sir John Soane's Museum.

The resource was co-designed through a series of workshops with young people. We are indebted for the contributions made by Key Stage 3 students at East Barnet School, London, and their teacher Elliot Richman and at St Benet Biscop Catholic Academy, Bedlington, facilitated by the Education Outreach officers at Newcastle University Library. Thanks are also due to the Soane Youth Panel.

Music for the project was arranged and performed by Nancy Kerr (violin, viola, viola da gamba, voice), Aisling Holmes (border pipes) and Matt Quinn (voice), and was recorded at Powered Flight Music. Kendra Packham selected, abridged and was historical adviser on the musical pieces.

Voices for the animations were provided by Bertie Fenton, Kerry Gooderson, Katie Guicciardi, Nancy Kerr, Daniel Lillie and Cameron Medford-Hawkins.
