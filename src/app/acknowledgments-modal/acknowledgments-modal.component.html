Acknowledgments

This online resource has been designed to present, explain and 'bring to life' the four paintings by William Hogarth that comprise his 'Humours of an Election' series. Painted in 1754-55, they have hung in the Picture Room of Sir John Soane's Museum in London since 1823.

This project was made possible by a Follow-On grant from the Arts and Humanities Research Council (AHRC), running from March to July 2025 ('Humours of an Election: Annotation, Animation, Education', AH/Z507556/1). It developed from an earlier AHRC grant, 'Eighteenth-Century Political Participation and Electoral Culture' (ECPPEC) that ran from 2020 to 2023, the main output of which can be found at https://ecppec.ncl.ac.uk/.

Both projects were led by Professor Matthew Grenby (Newcastle University), with co-investigators Professor Elaine Chalus (University of Liverpool) and Dr. Tom Schofield (Newcastle). Dr. Kendra Packham worked on the project as Senior Research and Innovation Associate, and Dan Foster-Smith as Research Software Developer (both Newcastle). Foster-Smith and Schofield developed the online platform, AI-generated animations and interactive functions. Chalus, Grenby and Packham wrote the annotations for the paintings. The scripts for the animated characters were written by Grenby and Packham, and sometimes quote from A Poetical Description of Mr Hogarth's Election Prints: in Four Cantos published in 1759, apparently 'under Mr Hogarth's Sanction and Inspection'.

The project was developed in partnership with Sir John Soane's Museum, with particular support from Jo Tinworth, Collections Curator (who has written extensive catalogue entries for the Soane's Hogarth paintings); Rhiannon Litterick, Public Programmes & Young People Manager; and Tallulah Smart, Museum Learning Officer. New, high-resolution digital images of the paintings were produced as part of the project. We are grateful for help with this photography, and for permission to use the images, from Sir John Soane's Museum.

Other original artworks used for this resource are in the public domain. This includes the image of William Hogarth used for the animated introduction, which has been adapted from an engraving of 'Hogarth Painting the Comic Muse', painted by Hogarth c.1757 and engraved in its final state in 1764.

The resource was co-designed through a series of workshops with young people. We are indebted for the contributions made by Key Stage 3 students and their teachers at East Barnet School, London (in particular Mr Elliot Richman) and St Benet Biscop Catholic Academy, Bedlington (facilitated by the Education Outreach officers at Newcastle University Library), and by members of the Youth Panel at Sir John Soane's Museum.

Music for the project was arranged and performed by Nancy Kerr (violin, viola, viola da gamba, voice), Aisling Holmes (border pipes) and Matt Quinn (voice), and was recorded at Powered Flight Music. Kendra Packham selected, abridged and was historical adviser on the musical pieces.

Voices for the animations were provided by Bertie Fenton, Kerry Gooderson, Katie Guicciardi, Nancy Kerr, Daniel Lille and Cameron Medford-Hawkins.
